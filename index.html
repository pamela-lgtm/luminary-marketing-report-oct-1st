Section Three

What else we heard this week

Becky follows up after every showing. Two conversations are worth passing on, and both are encouraging.

Prospect one — loved the home, one reservation

She said she loved the home and that everything was great. Her only hesitation was the layout: with bedrooms on the upper floor and one on the ground floor, a child would be two floors away from the rest of the family.

That is a genuine consideration for her household and not a criticism of the property. It is also the same three-storey layout that works extremely well for other households — a guest suite, an in-law space, a home office, or an older child who wants separation. Nothing to fix here. It simply means she is not the right fit, and the feature that lost her will win somebody else.

Prospect two — right home, wrong timing

Interested, but her move date is November 1. We asked her to check back in mid-October if the home is still available.

We are not counting her as an active prospect, because a home available now and a renter who cannot move for a month do not match. But she is on our list and we will reach out to her directly rather than waiting for her to remember us.

Across every showing in this campaign, not one prospect has raised the rent, the presentation or the condition of the home as a concern. The objections have all been about fit and timing — which is exactly what you want to hear at this price.

Section Four

Our recommendation

Hold at $2,995 while the application is in screening

No change this week, and no decision needed from you.

The price is working. Twelve showings and an application tell us the number is right, and reducing while a qualified applicant is in process would give away rent for no reason at all.

Keep showing the home as normal

This matters, and some owners are surprised by it. We do not take a home off the market because an application is in screening.

An application can fall away — screening can surface something, circumstances change, or an applicant finds another home while they wait. If we had stopped showing and the application did not hold, we would restart from a standing start having lost a week of the market's attention.

Showings continue until a lease is signed and a deposit is received. That is the only point at which a home is genuinely spoken for.

If the application is approved and a lease is signed, we will tell you immediately. If it is declined, we carry on without having lost any momentum — and the home will still be in front of every renter searching this week.

Your next report arrives Friday morning. In the meantime, Becky will keep you updated directly on the screening outcome and any further showings.
